Why Corporate Women Should Work Out (And How to Start)

By Maitrayee Bhowmick15 Nov 20265 min read

Back-to-back meetings, long commutes, deadlines, and then home and family. For many working women, exercise is the first thing to fall off the list. But the longer the hours you spend at a desk, the more your body needs you to move.

Here's what the research says, why it matters especially for women in corporate jobs, and a realistic way to fit workouts into a busy week.

The problem: Indian women are moving less than ever

A large study published in The Lancet Global Health in 2024 found that about 57% of Indian women don't get enough physical activity, compared with 42% of men. Desk jobs, long travel times and responsibilities at home all play a part.

The World Health Organization recommends that adults get 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week (like brisk walking), plus muscle-strengthening exercise on at least 2 days. A typical 9-to-6 office day makes that hard unless you plan for it.

What sitting all day does to your body

  • Stiff neck, shoulders and lower back from hours hunched over a laptop.
  • Weaker glutes and core, which affects posture and can lead to back pain.
  • Weight gain around the belly, which carries higher health risks for Indian women.
  • Higher blood sugar after meals when you sit for long stretches.
  • Low energy and an afternoon slump.

WHO's guidelines link high amounts of sitting with a greater risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers. The good news: even small changes help.

7 reasons working women should work out

1. Less stress, better mood

Physical activity is one of the most reliable ways to manage stress. Yoga and breathing practices are especially helpful for calming a busy mind after a demanding day.

2. Sharper focus at work

Many women find they think more clearly and feel less foggy on days they move. A short walk or a few stretches can reset your attention better than another cup of coffee.

3. Fewer aches and pains

Strengthening your back, core and hips and improving mobility can ease the neck and back pain that comes with long hours at a desk.

4. Better sleep

Regular exercise helps many people fall asleep more easily and sleep more deeply, and good sleep makes everything else easier.

5. Stronger bones and muscles for the years ahead

Women naturally lose muscle and bone density with age. Strength training in your 20s, 30s and 40s builds a reserve that protects you later.

6. Healthy weight and a smaller waist

Exercise, especially combined with strength training, helps you lose fat rather than muscle. Learn more in Weight Loss vs Fat Loss.

7. More energy, not less

It sounds backwards, but moving more usually leaves you with more energy for work and family, not less.

You don't need two hours at the gym. You need a plan that fits the life you already have.

How to fit exercise into a busy work week

Break up your sitting

Studies show that getting up for about 3 minutes every 30 minutes, to walk, climb stairs or do a few squats, can noticeably lower blood sugar spikes after meals compared with sitting straight through. Set a reminder on your phone or watch.

Try these desk-friendly moves

  • Neck rolls and shoulder circles
  • Seated spinal twist and side stretch
  • Chair squats: stand up and sit down 10 times
  • Calf raises while waiting for the lift or a call
  • A 5-minute walk after lunch

Plan short, focused sessions

Three or four 30-minute sessions a week can make a real difference. For example:

  • Monday and Thursday: 30 minutes of functional strength training
  • Tuesday and Friday: 30 minutes of yoga and pranayama
  • Weekend: a longer walk, a hike or a class you enjoy
  • Every day: movement breaks and a short post-lunch walk

Make it easy to show up

Schedule workouts in your calendar like meetings. Keep a mat at home. Online one-on-one sessions remove travel time completely, so you can train before work, at lunch or after the kids are asleep.

Don't forget food and sleep

Office life often means skipped breakfasts, canteen food and evening snacking. A little planning, like packing protein-rich snacks such as roasted chana or sprouts, goes a long way. And aim for 7 to 8 hours of sleep. It's part of your fitness, not separate from it.

Frequently asked questions

How much exercise do working women need?

WHO recommends 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity like brisk walking each week, plus muscle-strengthening exercise on at least 2 days. This can be split into short sessions across the week.

What exercises can I do at my office desk?

Neck rolls, shoulder circles, seated twists, chair squats and calf raises are all easy to do at work. Getting up for about 3 minutes every 30 minutes also helps.

Is 30 minutes of exercise a day enough?

For most people, 30 minutes on most days meets the basic WHO recommendation, especially if it includes strength training twice a week and you also move more during the day.

What is the best time to work out for working women?

The best time is the one you can stick to. Early mornings, lunch breaks or evenings all work. Online sessions can make it easier by removing travel time.

Can yoga help with work stress?

Yes. Yoga and breathing practices like pranayama can help calm the mind, ease physical tension from sitting and improve sleep.
