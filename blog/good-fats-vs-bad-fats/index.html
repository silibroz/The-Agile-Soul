Good Fats vs Bad Fats: A Simple Guide for Indian Kitchens

By Maitrayee Bhowmick15 Nov 20265 min read

Is ghee good or bad? Should you switch to olive oil? Is refined oil safe? Few topics in Indian kitchens cause as much confusion as fat. Here's the simple truth: your body needs fat, but the type and the amount make all the difference.

Why your body needs fat

Fat isn't the enemy. It gives you energy, helps your body absorb vitamins A, D, E and K, supports hormones and brain health, and makes food satisfying. Cutting fat out completely does more harm than good. The goal is to choose the right fats and keep the total in check.

The World Health Organization recommends that total fat stays under about 30% of your daily calories, with most of it coming from unsaturated fats.

The types of fat, explained simply

Unsaturated fats: the good fats

These are usually liquid at room temperature and are linked to better heart health when they replace saturated and trans fats.

  • Monounsaturated fats (MUFA): groundnut oil, sesame (til) oil, mustard oil, olive oil, almonds, peanuts.
  • Polyunsaturated fats (PUFA): sunflower, soybean and safflower oils, walnuts, flax seeds, sesame seeds.
  • Omega-3 fats are a special kind of PUFA. Vegetarian sources include flax seeds (alsi), walnuts, mustard oil, soybean, chia seeds, methi seeds and green leafy vegetables. Fish is the main non-veg source.

Saturated fats: fine in small amounts

Found in ghee, butter, cream, full-fat dairy, coconut oil, palm oil and red meat. You don't have to avoid them, but WHO advises keeping saturated fat to less than 10% of daily calories. ICMR-NIN's 2024 Dietary Guidelines for Indians also advise limiting high saturated-fat foods like ghee and butter.

Trans fats: the truly bad fat

Industrial trans fats are made when oils are partially hydrogenated, as in vanaspati, some bakery shortenings and margarines. They're also formed when oil is reheated again and again at high temperatures. Trans fats raise "bad" LDL cholesterol and lower "good" HDL cholesterol, and WHO says industrially produced trans fats are not part of a healthy diet at all.

Since January 2022, India's food regulator FSSAI has limited industrial trans fat to no more than 2% of the total fat in oils, fats and food products. That's a big step forward, but street food and reused frying oil can still be a problem.

No single oil is perfect. The healthiest kitchen uses a little of a few different oils, not a lot of one.

How much oil should you use in a day?

ICMR-NIN guidance suggests visible fat (cooking oil, ghee and butter) of roughly 20 to 50 g per person per day, depending on how active you are. For most women with desk jobs, the lower end, about 4 to 5 teaspoons, is plenty. Remember that nuts, seeds, dairy and packaged foods also contain "hidden" fat.

What about ghee?

Ghee has a place in Indian food and tradition, and a spoon on your dal or roti is fine for most healthy people. The problem is quantity. Because it's mostly saturated fat, the 2024 Indian guidelines suggest limiting it rather than treating it as a health food. Enjoy it for flavour, not by the ladle.

8 simple tips for healthier fats at home

  1. Rotate your oils. For example, use mustard or groundnut oil for one month or one dish, and til or soybean oil for another.
  2. Measure oil with a spoon instead of pouring from the bottle.
  3. Never reuse frying oil again and again.
  4. Choose steaming, roasting, grilling and sautéing over deep-frying.
  5. Add a spoon of ground flax seeds to atta, curd or smoothies.
  6. Snack on a small handful of nuts instead of namkeen or biscuits.
  7. Read labels and skip products listing "hydrogenated" or "partially hydrogenated" fats.
  8. Save fried food and rich mithai for occasions, not everyday meals.

Frequently asked questions

What are examples of good fats in Indian food?

Nuts like almonds, walnuts and peanuts, seeds like flax, til and pumpkin, and oils like mustard, groundnut, sesame and soybean oil, used in sensible amounts, are good sources of unsaturated fats.

Which fats are bad for health?

Industrial trans fats, found in vanaspati, some bakery shortenings and oil reused many times for frying, are the most harmful. Saturated fats like ghee, butter, coconut and palm oil are fine in small amounts but should be limited.

Is ghee healthy?

A small amount of ghee can be part of a healthy diet, but it is mostly saturated fat. India's 2024 dietary guidelines advise limiting ghee and butter rather than using them freely.

Which cooking oil is best for Indian cooking?

No single oil is perfect. Using a variety, such as mustard, groundnut, sesame and soybean oil, in moderate amounts gives a better balance of fatty acids than relying on one oil.

How much oil should I eat per day?

ICMR-NIN guidance suggests roughly 20 to 50 g of visible fat a day depending on activity. For most less active women, around 4 to 5 teaspoons is enough.
